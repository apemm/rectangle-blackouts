\section{Computations and verification}\label{sec:verify}

The code and the stored results are in the repository~\cite{repo}. From its
root, the scripts are in \nolinkurl{jis/scripts} and the evidence files in
\nolinkurl{jis/data}, and \nolinkurl{jis/scripts/README.md} gives the commands.
The ancillary files of this paper are in \nolinkurl{papers/enumeration/anc}.
Every computed count has $2$ separately written checks.

\paragraph{Three rows.} The proofs in Section~\ref{sec:three} are by hand up to
the expansion of rational functions, which we did with SymPy~\cite{sympy2017}.
One program finds all optima of the
$3\times m$ grid from Definition~\ref{def:valid} for $m\le10$, and its counts
agree with~\eqref{eq:PQ}. A transfer matrix built from Table~\ref{tab:windows}
agrees with~\eqref{eq:PQ} and~\eqref{eq:Eclosed} for $m\le100$. For
Theorem~\ref{thm:threeorbits}, the fixed words are listed for $m\le24$ and the
orbits for $m\le10$. The files are \nolinkurl{three_row_*.json}.

\paragraph{Four rows.} The ancillary script \nolinkurl{four_row_certificate.py}
builds the automata of Proposition~\ref{prop:fourautomaton} and carries out the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:fourgfexact} in about $5$ minutes. It needs only
\nolinkurl{four_row_gf.txt}. The repository also holds an earlier computation
that does not use this script (\nolinkurl{four_row_*.json}). It works with
smaller deterministic machines found by a search, checks on the product
automaton that they accept the same words as $\mathcal A_F$, and gives the
same counts for all $m\le1500$ with $2$ transfer counters. It also provides the
series of the $3$ pair sets and the isolation of the roots of $Q_4$.

\paragraph{Five rows and small grids.} The files for Table~\ref{tab:fivevalues}
are \nolinkurl{five_row_count_*.json} and \nolinkurl{five_row_optima_dp_w*.json}.
The counts $a_4(4)$, $a_4(5)$, $a_5(5)$, $a_5(6)$, and $a_5(7)$ of
Table~\ref{tab:values} come from complete enumerations, each done by $2$
programs (\nolinkurl{verify_narrow.py} and \nolinkurl{table_counts}). The SAT
enumerations use PySAT~\cite{pysat2018}. Each blackout found is tested again
against Definition~\ref{def:valid}.

\paragraph{Formal proofs.} The folder \nolinkurl{lean} of the repository
contains proofs in Lean of Lemmas~\ref{lem:diff}, \ref{lem:threecorners},
\ref{lem:span}, and~\ref{lem:girth}, of the Strip Theorem with its count, and of
the example after Theorem~\ref{thm:hidden}.
