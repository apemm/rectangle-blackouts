\section{Five rows and small grids}\label{sec:five}

Let $m\ge8$. Then $f(5,m)=m+6=m+q_5$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:imports}(c). As on $4$
rows, Theorem~\ref{thm:imports}(b) describes the optima. An optimum has $6$
doubles and $m-6$ singletons, and the row pairs of the doubles are the $6$ edges
of a complete bipartite graph with parts of sizes $2$ and $3$ on the rows. There
are $10$ such graphs, so there are $10\,(m)_6\,5^{m-6}$ words of this form. Each
of them is axis-valid and hides no axis-aligned rectangle. A tilted rectangle
has span at most $W_5=5$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:span}. As in
Lemma~\ref{lem:fourmemory}, validity can then be tested on windows of $11$
consecutive columns. We have no analogue of Theorem~\ref{thm:hidden}, so the
test must also remember whether a tilted rectangle has been hidden. We did not
build the automaton. Two separately written programs count the valid words by
a transfer over the columns.

\begin{proposition}[five-row counts, computer-assisted]\label{prop:fivecounts}
The numbers $a_5(m)$ for $8\le m\le23$ are those of Table~\ref{tab:fivevalues}
\textup{(}\seqnum{A401225}\textup{)}. The $4$ optima of the $5\times8$ grid are the
blackout with the column word $14,45,25,5,5,34,23,12$, where each entry lists
the black rows of a column, and its images under the row reflection and the
column reversal.
\end{proposition}

The evidence is as follows. One transfer counter gives all $16$ values. A second
one, written separately, gives the same values for $13\le m\le23$. For
$8\le m\le12$ the values agree with complete enumerations by a SAT solver and by
a column search, and at $m=8$ also with a test of all
$10\,(8)_6\,5^2=5040000$ words of the form above.

Table~\ref{tab:values} contains $9$ more counts for grids with both sides at
least $4$, from $a_4(4)=316$ to $a_7(7)=7300$. On these grids the largest size
is below the row bound, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:imports}(b) does not apply. The counts come from complete
enumerations, each done by $2$ separately written programs
(Section~\ref{sec:verify}). On the $7\times8$ grid one enumeration lists $18$ optima,
which proves only $a_7(8)\ge18$. The counts drop where $f(h,m)-m$ increases,
for example from $a_5(7)=1184$ to $a_5(8)=4$.
