\section{Every fixed height}\label{sec:height}

Fix the height $h$. The letters are now all subsets of $\{1,\dots,h\}$, and a
blackout of the $h\times m$ grid is the column word $w=B_1\cdots B_m$. Write
$|w|=m$ for its length and $S(w)$ for the blackout. Let $\mathcal L_h$ be the
set of words of valid blackouts, of all lengths. This section does not use the
companion paper.

\begin{theorem}[regularity]\label{thm:regular}
For every $h\ge1$ the language $\mathcal L_h$ is regular. Hence the series
$\sum_{w\in\mathcal L_h}z^{|w|}u^{|S(w)|}$ is a rational function of $z$ and $u$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The \emph{trace} of a rectangle of the $h\times m$ grid is the word
$Y_1\cdots Y_m$, where $Y_i$ is the set of rows of its corners in column $i$.
Different rectangles have different traces. By Lemma~\ref{lem:diff}, the word
$w$ is not valid if and only if there are $2$ different traces $Y$ and $Y'$ of
length $m$ with $Y_i\triangle Y'_i\subseteq B_i$ for every $i$.

The traces of all lengths form a regular language. The trace of an axis-aligned
rectangle is a letter $\{r,s\}$ with $r\ne s$, then some empty letters, then the
same letter again, with any number of empty letters before and after. By
Lemmas~\ref{lem:leftmost} and~\ref{lem:span}, the trace of a tilted rectangle is
one of finitely many words of length at most $W_h+1$, again with empty letters
before and after.

Read a triple $(w,Y,Y')$ of words of the same length as one word whose letters
are triples of sets. The triples in which $Y$ and $Y'$ are different traces and
$Y_i\triangle Y'_i\subseteq B_i$ for every $i$ form a regular language.
Forgetting $Y$ and $Y'$ keeps it regular. So the words that are not valid form
a regular language, and $\mathcal L_h$ is its complement.

Take a deterministic automaton for $\mathcal L_h$ and give the step that reads
$B$ the weight $zu^{|B|}$. Let $T(z,u)$ be the matrix of these weights, $\alpha$
the indicator row vector of the start state, and $\beta$ the indicator column
vector of the accepting states. Each word of $\mathcal L_h$ has exactly $1$
accepting path, so the series is $\alpha(I-T(z,u))^{-1}\beta$. This is rational
by Cramer's rule~\cite[Proposition~V.6]{flajolet2009}.
\end{proof}

The \emph{excess} of a word is $e(w)=|S(w)|-|w|$, so that $e(uv)=e(u)+e(v)$ and
$f(h,m)-m$ is the largest excess of a word of length $m$ in $\mathcal L_h$.

\begin{theorem}[fixed height]\label{thm:rational}
Let $h\ge1$.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item For every integer $k$ the words of $\mathcal L_h$ with excess $k$ form a
regular language.
\item The sequence $f(h,m)-m$ is ultimately periodic in $m$.
\item The column words of the optima of the grids with $h$ rows form a regular
language, and $\sum_{m\ge1}a_h(m)z^m$ is a rational function.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) We first bound the excess of a valid word $v$. If $|v|\le2$, then
$e(v)\le2h-2$. Let $|v|\ge3$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:girth} no $2$ columns share $2$
black rows, so $\sum_i\binom{|B_i|}2\le\binom h2$. Since
$|B_i|-1\le\binom{|B_i|}2$, we get $e(v)\le\binom h2$. Put
$c_h=\max\bigl(\binom h2,2h-2\bigr)$. Every factor of a valid word is the word
of a valid blackout of a smaller grid. Now let $w=uv$ be valid with $e(w)=k$.
Then
\[
k-c_h\le e(u)=k-e(v)\le c_h .
\]
So an automaton for $\mathcal L_h$ can also keep the excess of the prefix read
so far. It rejects when this value leaves the interval $[k-c_h,c_h]$, and at
the end it accepts when the value is $k$. This is a finite automaton for the
words of $\mathcal L_h$ with excess $k$.

(b) Let $\Lambda_k$ be the set of lengths of the words in (a). Replacing every
letter by the same symbol turns a regular language into a regular language
over $1$ letter, and a deterministic automaton over $1$ letter is a path that
ends in a cycle. So each $\Lambda_k$ is ultimately periodic. The blackout that
consists of row $1$ is valid. Indeed a tilted rectangle has only $1$ corner in
its top row, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:leftmost}. So a rectangle with fewer than $3$
white corners is axis-aligned with a side in row $1$, and its $2$ white corners
determine it. Hence $f(h,m)-m$ is the largest $k$ with $0\le k\le c_h$ and
$m\in\Lambda_k$. These are finitely many ultimately periodic sets, so
$f(h,m)-m$ is ultimately periodic.

(c) For each $k$ let $D_k$ be the set of all $m\ge1$ with $f(h,m)-m=k$. It is
ultimately periodic by (b), so the words with length in $D_k$ form a regular
language. The words of the optima are the words of $\mathcal L_h$ with excess
$k$ and length in $D_k$, for $0\le k\le c_h$. This is a finite union of regular
languages. Its series is rational, as in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:regular}.
\end{proof}

By the companion paper~\cite{pemmasaniExtremal}, the sequence $f(h,m)-m$ is in
fact eventually constant, equal to $q_h$ for $h\ge3$. For the largest excess
that occurs, part (a) also follows from a result of Guilhot, Little, and
Parkinson~\cite[Theorem~1(3)]{glp2025} on additive weights that are bounded
above on a regular language.
