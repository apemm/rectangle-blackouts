# Two split primes: the largest exponent is free

13 November 2026. Problem 001. Status labels as in the other notes of this folder: nothing here is in the paper. "Audit" means a separate line-by-line check by a second reader who derived the critical steps again.

Result: chi(Gamma_S) for two split primes is still undecided. Theorem W: chi_inf(T) is bounded independently of the largest split exponent, for any number of split primes, with growth about m^1.24 in the smaller exponent for {11, 19}. Proposition B and Corollary B1: per-level digit counting cannot do better than m/10 colours. Proposition D: a uniform Bohr bound exists exactly when the slab is not a set of Bohr recurrence. # Result

## Status

partial

## Summary

The question is still open: chi(Gamma_S) is not decided for {11, 19} or any other pair. I proved three things. Theorem W: chi_inf(T) is bounded independently of the largest split exponent, for any number of split primes, uniformly in the primes; for two primes it grows like m^1.24 for {11, 19}, where m is the smaller exponent, against m^2.55 in note (E). Proposition B: the per-level digit counting behind Lemma D, (E) and W cannot do better than m/10 colours, so a poly(log m) bound needs a genuinely new colouring. Proposition D: a Bohr bound uniform in both exponents exists exactly when the slab is not a set of Bohr recurrence.

## Statements

Notation follows gamma_one_split_prime.md as corrected by its audit.
- G_S = Z_(2) ∩ Z[1/(5 prod S)].
- D^Sigma is the slab {±r(beta) : sigma1(beta) in Sigma, r(beta) in Z_(2), r(beta) ≠ 0}.
- k = (k_l) is the exponent vector of beta = ±eps^n prod omega_l^{k_l}, and box(e) = {|k_l| <= e_l}.
- For odd T = (inert part)·5^a·prod_{i=1}^w l_i^{e_i}, index the split primes so that e_1 <= e_2 <= ... <= e_w, and put rho_{<i} = prod_{i'<i} (2e_{i'}+1), with rho_{<1} = 1.
- For a prime l and M >= 1, K_l(M) = min{N^t : N >= 3, t >= 1, M·ceil(2l/N)^t < l^t}.
- F_w = min(20^(2^w), (20(w+1))^(w+1)).

LEMMA Dt (t-character digit lemma) [proved]. Fix a split prime l in S and sets E_j (j >= 1) of nonzero r in G_S with v_l(r) = -j and at most M elements up to sign. If M·ceil(2l/N)^t < l^t, there are zeta^(1), ..., zeta^(t) in Z_l such that every r in the union of the E_j has max_s ||{zeta^(s) r}_l|| >= 1/N.

LEMMA K (cost of Dt) [proved, elementary].
- (a) K_l(M) <= (2l)^(floor(log_l M) + 1), and hence K_l(M) <= 2l·M^(log(2l)/log l).
- (b) K_l(M) <= 4M + 2 if l >= 2M + 1.
- (c) K_l(M) <= 6 M^2.29 for every l >= 11 and M >= 1.
- (d) K_l(4) <= 18 for l >= 11. Exact values: K_11(4) = 11, K_19(4) = 10, K_19(12) = 38, K_11(12) = 64.

THEOREM W (the largest exponent is free, any w, uniform in the primes) [proved from audited inputs]. For every h:
 chi(G_T(h)) <= chi_inf(T) <= F_w · prod_{i=1}^{w} K_{l_i}(4 rho_{<i}).
The bound does not depend on e_w, on a, or on the inert part. It is at most F_w·18·prod_{i=2}^{w} 6(4 rho_{<i})^2.29 for all split primes.
- w = 1: 400·K_l(4) <= 7200, and N = 16 as in Lemma D gives back Theorem E's 6400.
- w = 2, with m = e_1 <= e_2: chi_inf(T) <= 160000·K_{l_1}(4)·K_{l_2}(8m+4) <= 2.9·10^6·K_{l_2}(8m+4). That is O(m^1.236) if the larger exponent sits on l_2 = 19, O(m^1.289) if it sits on 11, and at most 1.8·10^7·m^2.29 uniformly. Example: chi_inf(11·19^e·5^a·inert) <= 160000·11·38 = 6.7·10^7 for every e. Note (E) gives 4.5·10^9 here, with growth m^2.55.

PROPOSITION B (column averaging) [proved under hypothesis (Hfull)]. Take S = {p, q}. Hypothesis (Hfull): the closed subgroup of Z_p^* generated by iota'(phi^2) and iota'(s_q omega_q) is all of Z_p^*. Here iota' is the embedding of K at the place above p where pi_p is a unit, and s_q = sign sigma1(omega_q). Computer check: (Hfull) holds for (p, q) = (11, 19), (11, 29), (29, 11), (19, 29) and (29, 19); for (19, 11) the subgroup has index 2.
Let zeta_1, ..., zeta_t be in Z_p, N >= 2 and m >= 1. Call a slab element with sigma1(beta) in (0.8, 1.2), k_p in [1, m] and k_q = j >= 1 p-unseparated if ||{zeta_s r}_p|| < 1/N for every s. Then for infinitely many q-levels j (indeed on a set of j of positive lower density), at least
 c(m) = 0.4213·((1 - 1/p) m / N^t - 1/p)
distinct p-unseparated slab elements have k_q = j.

COROLLARY B1 (method barrier) [proved]. Consider any argument that colours the slab of box(m, e), with e >= the levels used, by t pure p-characters, and treats the q-side by per-level counting (Lemma Dt with t' characters and N' cells at q). It uses at least m/10 colours. So per-level counting with separable characters cannot give poly(log m). A bound poly(log m) needs characters mixing p and q, or a non-Bohr colouring.

PROPOSITION D (Bohr dichotomy) [proved]. Let beta_B(m, e) be the least prod N_k over finite families (chi_k, N_k) of characters of G_S such that every r in D^Sigma ∩ box(m, e) has some k with ||chi_k(r)|| >= 1/N_k. Then sup_{m,e} beta_B(m, e) < infinity iff D^Sigma is not a set of Bohr recurrence, iff Gamma_S has a Bohr colouring. By Theorem W, beta_B(m, e) <= K_{l_1}(4)·K_{l_2}(8m+4) for every e >= m.

WHAT BOHR RECURRENCE WOULD IMPLY [proved implications, unproved hypothesis]. If D^Sigma is a set of Bohr recurrence:
- Gamma_S has no Bohr colouring.
- beta_B(m, e) tends to infinity as min(m, e) tends to infinity. By Corollary B1 it is at most polynomial in min(m, e), but its growth rate is unknown.
- chi(Gamma_S) is still undecided (Katznelson's question), and any finite colouring would have to be non-Bohr.
- chi_inf(T) stays polynomial in the smaller exponent (Theorem W).

NOT PROVED:
- chi(Gamma_S) < infinity or = infinity for any pair.
- Bohr recurrence or non-recurrence of D^Sigma.
- A bound poly(w, log e_small) for chi(G_T).

## Proofs

Inputs, all audited in audit_gamma_one_split_prime.md:
- Theorem 1(c): v_l(r) = -|k_l|.
- Theorem F: the off-strip identities chi_{-sigma}(r) = f_A(s) and chi_sigma(r) = f_C(s).
- Lemma B: F(s) >= sqrt(2 - 2/sqrt5) - 1 = 0.05146 off Sigma.
- Lemma C, multi-prime form: at most 2 slab elements per k up to sign; strip elements with k = 0 give r = 0.
- L2 and T2: Cay(Z, D_T^inf) ≅ Gamma[(1/T*)Z].
- chi(G_T(h)) <= chi_inf(T), because D_T(h) ⊂ D_T^inf.

[Dt] Choose the base-l digits of the t parameters level by level. Let r be in E_j, so r = u l^(-j) with u in Z_(l)^*. Then {zeta r}_l = (zeta u mod l^j)/l^j depends only on zeta mod l^j. Suppose the digits below position j-1 are fixed and the digit d at position j-1 runs over Z/l. Then zeta u changes by d·u_0·l^(j-1) mod l^j, where u_0 = u mod l is nonzero. So the values form one coset c + (1/l)Z/Z. An open arc of length 2/N contains at most ceil(2l/N) points of such a coset. Hence for each r, at most ceil(2l/N)^t of the l^t digit vectors at position j-1 leave r unseparated. Elements at level j do not depend on later digits. With at most M elements up to sign, and ±r having equal norms, the strict inequality leaves a good vector at every level.

[K] (a) Take N = 2l. Then ceil(2l/N) = 1, and the condition is M < l^t, so t = floor(log_l M) + 1 works. This gives (2l)^t <= 2l·M^(log 2l/log l).
(b) Take N = 4M+2 and t = 1. Then ceil(2l/N) = ceil(l/(2M+1)) <= q + 1 with q = floor(l/(2M+1)) >= 1. And l >= 2Mq + q > M(q+1), so the condition holds.
(c) If l >= 2M+1, use (b). Otherwise use (a): 2l <= 4M, and log(2l)/log l <= log 22/log 11 = 1.2891. So K <= 4M·M^1.29 <= 6M^2.29.
(d) By (b) with M = 4. The exact values come from kvals.py.

[W] Coloring. Colour x in (1/T*)Z ∩ Z_(2) by two parts.
- Part 1: the cells of the off-strip characters. Either floor(20{chi_z(x)}) for all z in {±1}^w, giving 20^(2^w) colours, or the Theorem F cells of side 1/(20(w+1)) in (rho, lambda_1, ..., lambda_w).
- Part 2: for each i and s <= t_i, the cell floor(N_i {zeta_i^(s) x}_{l_i}).

Choice of the zeta. Assign each slab element r with exponent vector k ≠ 0 in box(e) to the prime l_{i*}, where i* = max{i : k_i ≠ 0}, at level j = |k_{i*}|. It has v_{l_{i*}}(r) = -j by Theorem 1(c). At (i, j) the vectors k have k_i = ±j, k_{i'} = 0 for i' > i, and |k_{i'}| <= e_{i'} for i' < i. There are 2 rho_{<i} of them, each with at most 2 elements up to sign (Lemma C). So the load is at most M_i = 4 rho_{<i}. Lemma Dt gives zeta_i^(s) with N_i^(t_i) = K_{l_i}(M_i). Only the digits up to level e_i are used, so e_w never enters.

Properness. Suppose x and x + d have the same colour, with d = ±r(beta) an edge, so |k_i| <= e_i.
- If sigma1(beta) is not in Sigma: every chi_z(d) is below 1/20 in norm (in the Theorem F version, the sum of w+1 cell widths is below 1/20). But max(||chi_sigma||, ||chi_{-sigma}||) = F(s) > 0.0514 > 1/20, a contradiction.
- If sigma1(beta) is in Sigma: k ≠ 0, and d is assigned to some (i*, j). Some zeta has ||{zeta d}|| >= 1/N_{i*}, while equal cells force a norm below 1/N_{i*}, a contradiction.

Then L2 and T2 give the bound for chi_inf(T), and chi(G_T(h)) <= chi_inf(T). For w = 2, M_1 = 4 and M_2 = 4(2m+1).

[B] Fix p and q with k_p = i in [1, m], k_q = j >= 1, and the window (0.8, 1.2). The slab condition is n + i a_p + j a_q in W' = (log 0.8, log 1.2)/(2 log phi), with a_l = log|sigma1 omega_l|/(2 log phi).

The phase variable. Write phi_j = {j a_q}. The element (i, j) exists iff the interval W' - i a_p - phi_j (length 0.4213 < 1) contains an integer tau_i(phi_j). Then n = tau_i(phi_j) - floor(j a_q).

The unit p^i r. At P (pi_p ≡ 0), v_P(beta) = i, and at P', v_{P'}(beta) = -i. So p^i beta is p-integral, iota_P(phi p^i beta) ≡ 0 mod p^i, and
 p^i r = L(p^i beta) - p^i/10 ≡ iota'(phi)/10 · iota'(sg eps^n pi_p^(2i) omega_q^j) (mod p^i).
The sign is sg = (-1)^n s_p^i s_q^j, so sg·eps^n = (phi^2)^n s_p^i s_q^j. Hence p^i r ≡ h_i(phi_j)·V(j) (mod p^i), where:
- h_i(phi) is a p-unit that depends only on i and tau_i(phi);
- V(j) = iota'(phi^2)^(-floor(j a_q))·iota'(s_q omega_q)^j.
For c ≡ p^i r mod p^i, the element is unseparated iff c lies in Bad_i = {u mod p^i : ||{zeta_s u/p^i}|| < 1/N for all s}.

Counting Bad_i. Let b_i = |Bad_i|, counting all residues.
- Pigeonhole: put the p^i points ({zeta_s u/p^i})_s into N^t half-open boxes of side 1/N. Some box holds at least p^i/N^t of them, and their differences with one fixed member lie in Bad_i. So b_i >= p^i/N^t.
- The non-units of Bad_i are u = p v with v in Bad_{i-1}, so Bad_i has b_i - b_{i-1} units. Its density among units is dens_i >= (b_i - b_{i-1})/p^i.
- Abel summation with b_0 = 1: sum_{i<=m} dens_i >= (1 - 1/p) sum_{i<m} b_i/p^i + b_m/p^m - 1/p >= (1 - 1/p) m/N^t - 1/p.

Equidistribution. Let Pm = |(Z/p^m)^*|.
- V(j) mod p^m and phi_j are functions of (j mod Pm, {j a_q/Pm}), with floor(j a_q) mod Pm and {j a_q} both read off from {j a_q/Pm}.
- a_q is irrational: otherwise some ±omega_q^v eps^(-u) would have |sigma1| = |sigma2| = 1, forcing (pi_q)^v = (pi_q')^v as ideals.
- By Weyl, (j/Pm, j a_q/Pm) is equidistributed in (1/Pm)Z/Z × T. So (j mod Pm, floor(j a_q) mod Pm, phi_j) is uniform on (Z/Pm)^2 × [0, 1).
- The homomorphism (c1, c2) -> iota'(phi^2)^(-c1) iota'(s_q omega_q)^(c2) pushes the uniform measure to Haar measure on its image, which is all of (Z/p^m)^* under (Hfull) (generation mod p^2 suffices for odd p). Translation by h_i^(-1) preserves Haar measure.

So the column count C(j) = sum_{i<=m} 1[present_i(phi_j)]·1[h_i V(j) ∈ Bad_i] has mean value sum_i 0.4213·dens_i >= c(m). C is integer valued and its Cesàro averages converge to this mean, so C(j) >= c(m) for infinitely many j. Different i give different k, hence distinct elements.

[B1] Let X = N^t.
- If X >= m/10, there are already at least m/10 colours.
- Otherwise c(m) >= 0.4213(0.909m/X - 1/11) > 0, and the q-side at a column from Proposition B must satisfy c(m)·beta'^(t') < q^(t'), with beta' = ceil(2q/N'). Since N' >= 2q/beta', its cost is N'^(t') >= (2q/beta')^(t') > 2^(t')·c(m). The total is at least 2X·c(m) >= 0.84(0.909m - X/11) > 0.75m.

[D] Suppose D^Sigma is not a set of Bohr recurrence: some chi_1, ..., chi_K and delta have max_k ||chi_k(r)|| >= delta on all of D^Sigma. Then beta_B <= ceil(1/delta)^K for every box.
Conversely, suppose beta_B <= C for all boxes. Then K <= log2 C and each N_k <= C. Some type (K, N_1, ..., N_K) occurs along boxes exhausting Z^2, and hence for all boxes, since the conditions are nested. For each box, the set of K-tuples in (dual of G_S)^K that separate it is closed, and these sets decrease. The dual is compact, so the intersection is nonempty, and that family separates all of D^Sigma. With the off-strip cells, this is a Bohr colouring of Gamma_S. A Bohr colouring of Gamma_S restricts to D^Sigma ⊂ D_S, so it gives non-recurrence. Characters of the subgroups (1/T)Z extend to G_S because T is divisible.

[Why the question stays open: analysis, not proof] Bohr recurrence would need a slab element (i, j) whose p-condition, a constraint on V(j) mod p^i, and q-condition, a constraint on V'(i) mod q^j, hold at the same time.
- Proposition B makes p-bad rows plentiful only at columns j with j of order p^m or more.
- The q-condition at such a column needs the rows' residues mod q^j to be spread, which needs row ranges of order q^j.
- This scale coupling defeats every averaging or greedy argument I tried. A single p-character can be defeated greedily (digit by digit), but the q-side then faces the same coupled problem.
- In the other direction, a designer has one digit per row and per column. Proposition B shows that per-level counting cannot absorb the linear load. Only coordinated carries could, and I found no mechanism.

## Gaps

- The main question is open. chi(Gamma_S) is not decided for {11, 19} or for any pair. Neither a Bohr colouring with l-adic parameters outside Q + Q s5_l nor a proof that D^Sigma is a set of Bohr recurrence was found.
- The requested weaker target, chi(G_T) <= poly(w, log e_small) uniformly in the larger exponents, is not reached. Theorem W is uniform only in the single largest exponent, and it is polynomial in the smaller exponents: degree about 1.24 to 1.29 for w = 2, and a product of degrees for larger w. Corollary B1 shows that per-level counting cannot go below m/10 here, so poly(log m) needs a new mechanism.
- The off-strip factor F_w = min(20^(2^w), (20(w+1))^(w+1)) is superexponential in w. So Theorem W is worse than chi_orbit_bound Theorem 2 (C rho_s log rho_s) for squarefree T with many split primes, which is the regime that drives the board's B = (log h)^1.5. Theorem W does not improve Theorem N or the label-scaffold exponent; it helps only diagonals with one dominant prime power.
- Proposition B needs hypothesis (Hfull). It is checked by computer mod p^2 for (p,q) = (11,19), (11,29), (29,11), (19,29), (29,19) and fails (index 2) for (19,11); the coset version is unproved. Corollary B1 is a barrier for one proof method (pure p-characters plus per-level counting at q), not a lower bound on all Bohr colourings.
- Proposition B's elementary pigeonhole bound b_i >= p^i/N^t is a factor of about 2 below the van der Corput bound. I did not use van der Corput.
- Nothing here is audited. The inputs (Theorem 1(c), Theorem F, Lemmas B and C, L2, T2) are audited. Theorem W replaces the unaudited (E) of gamma_two_split_primes.md, so the audit should target W directly.
- The 'why open' paragraph (scale coupling) is analysis, not proof.

## Missing estimate

For S = {p, q}, either outcome needs a statement about the slab elements (k_p, k_q) = (i, j).

The p-adic data of element (i, j) is the residue V(j) mod p^i together with the phase {j a_q}. The q-adic data is V'(i) mod q^j together with {i a_p}. Here V(j) = iota'(phi^2)^(-floor(j a_q)) iota'(s_q omega_q)^j, and V' is the same with p and q swapped.

- Bohr recurrence (no Bohr colouring) needs this. For every finite set of p-adic and q-adic characters, and every delta > 0, there is (i, j) on which all characters are below delta. Proposition B gives many p-bad rows at a positive density of columns, but only at columns j of order p^m or more. What is missing is joint equidistribution, a statement of 'transversality' or Furstenberg ×p ×q type: for those columns, the residues V'(i) mod q^j over the p-bad rows must be spread.
- A Bohr colouring needs characters mixing both primes whose carries are coordinated, so that the linear load per level from Proposition B is absorbed without per-level counting.
- For the weaker target, poly(log m), one needs the same coordination for the truncated box |k_p| <= m.
