# Planting good anchors with uniformly bounded prefix error

15 November 2026. A deterministic good-row family can be planted in both
classes of a mechanical or multiscale binary partition. Disjoint local
balanced swaps change every prefix count by at most one. Consequently
the bounded-error inverse-count equations used by the sampling kernels
survive, while both classes inherit the family's gap moments.

This transfers a physical-row gap theorem to sampled raw indices. It
does not prove the needed physical gap theorem for every profile or a
P-valid singleton assignment. Geometric constants and row filters must
be fixed for the enlarged class-gap bound before applying the result.

## 1. Binary partition and deterministic anchor positions

Let \(w(0),\ldots,w(N-1)\in\{0,1\}\) be a binary word such
that the occurrence lists of both symbols have adjacent gaps at most
three, and every index outside a fixed endpoint buffer lies within
distance three of each symbol. The second condition explicitly controls
the initial and terminal runs; internal occurrence gaps alone would not
do so. These conditions hold for the reviewed count-function partitions
with derivative between \(1/3\) and \(2/3\). Reserve an endpoint
buffer of width at least three.

Let \(G\) be a deterministic set of good physical row-pool indices.
Thin it greedily to a sequence
\(z_1<z_2<\cdots\) with consecutive spacings at least ten: take
the first available index, then the least next good index at least ten
farther on. Prescribe alternating desired symbols at these anchors,
for example zero at odd-numbered anchors and one at even-numbered ones.
The thinning and desired symbols are fixed before the word is sampled.

At an anchor already carrying its desired symbol, do nothing. Otherwise
find an occurrence of the desired symbol within distance three in the
original word, using a fixed tie rule, and swap the two bits. Such an
occurrence exists by the class-gap assumption and the endpoint buffer.
Each swap is supported within \([z_j-3,z_j+3]\). These neighborhoods
are disjoint, so the swaps do not interfere and all anchors end with
their prescribed symbols.

Every physical index still belongs to exactly one class. The size of
each class is unchanged. In particular, this does not duplicate or
delete any original row label.

## 2. Prefix counts and occurrence gaps

Write \(W(t)=\sum_{0\le n<t}w(n)\), and let \(W'(t)\) be the
new prefix count. One swap contributes zero unless its two positions
straddle the prefix boundary; when they do, its contribution is one
or minus one. The support intervals of distinct swaps are disjoint,
so at most one swap straddles any given boundary. Therefore
\[
                              |W'(t)-W(t)|\le1
                              \quad\hbox{for every }t.          \tag{1}
\]
The zero-symbol prefix count has the same bound, since it is \(t-W(t)\).

Each symbol's old positions can be matched to its new positions with
displacement at most three: match unchanged positions to themselves and
the two swapped symbol occurrences to their new positions. Sorting these
matches cannot increase the maximum displacement. Hence, for either
symbol's ordered occurrence lists,
\[
                             |p'_i-p_i|\le3.         \tag{2}
\]
In particular the new adjacent gaps are at most \(3+6=9\). The
precise constant is not important; its independence of \(N\) is.

If the original word is generated by a counting function \(C\),
then \(W(t)=C(t)+O(1)\). Equation (1) shows
\[
 W'(t)=C(t)+O(1),\qquad
 C(p_i^{\prime,1})=i+O(1),\qquad
 p_i^{\prime,0}-C(p_i^{\prime,0})=i+O(1),           \tag{3}
\]
with uniform errors. Index conventions change only their fixed constants.
The selected swaps can depend on the sampled word: (1)--(3) are
pointwise identities or uniform inequalities for every word.

## 3. Good-gap moments survive thinning and alternating colors

Fix \(\gamma\ge1\). Include the boundary gaps of the physical
interval under consideration, or bracket it by good indices from the
full good family. Let \(H_\gamma(G)\) denote the sum of the
\(\gamma\)-th powers of the consecutive good-index gaps so obtained.

For consecutive greedily retained anchors \(z_j,z_{j+1}\), let
\(q_j\) be the last original good index below \(z_j+10\).
Then \(0\le q_j-z_j\le9\), and \(z_{j+1}\) is the original
good index immediately following \(q_j\). Thus
\[
                     z_{j+1}-z_j\le9+(z_{j+1}-q_j). \tag{4}
\]
Different retained gaps use different original gaps in (4).
Convexity therefore bounds the retained-anchor moment by
\[
                         C_\gamma\bigl(N+H_\gamma(G)\bigr).
                         \tag{5}
\]
The finitely many endpoint-buffer losses fit the same bound.

Each symbol receives every second retained anchor. Its consecutive
planted-anchor gaps are sums of at most two retained gaps, apart from
the same bounded endpoint convention. Another use of convexity gives
the bound (5) for each symbol separately. Hence a good-family estimate
\(H_\gamma(G)\le F(N)\), with \(F(N)\ge N\), transfers to
the planted anchors of both classes with only a constant factor.

Between two planted anchors of a class at physical indices \(u<v\),
their occurrence-index distance is at most \(v-u\). Every bad raw
run of that class lies between planted good anchors. The sum of the
lengths of all such runs inside one anchor gap is at most \(v-u\),
so the sum of their \(\gamma\)-th powers is at most \((v-u)^\gamma\).
It follows that
\[
       \sum_{\text{bad raw runs in either one class}}\ell^\gamma
                 \le C_\gamma\bigl(N+H_\gamma(G)\bigr).         \tag{6}
\]
This is the step that is unavailable for an unmodified sampled class,
which could skip isolated good physical rows.

For example, a physical good family with gap moment
\(N(\log N)^{(3/4)(\gamma-1)}\), for every fixed \(\gamma<3/2\),
gives the same raw-run moment after planting. Its squared-run consequence
remains \(N^{4/3+\eta}\) for every fixed \(\eta>0\); planting
does not improve that exponent.

## 4. Compatibility with the independent sampling proof

Apply the operation separately to each parity field and, in the V-map
model, separately to each independently sampled arm. It is a deterministic
map of that field's word, so it creates no dependence between independent
parity or arm fields. The underlying dyadic coefficients themselves are
unchanged and remain independent.

For prescribed target rows, equations (3) are the same count equations
used by the reviewed multiscale kernels, with a larger fixed error.
Those arguments bound events by fixed-width strips in selected independent
coefficients. They do not require the occurrence error to be independent
of those coefficients. Thus adaptive local choices of swap partners do
not invalidate the point-mass argument: every realization still lies in
the same deterministically enlarged strips. The fixed-target proof and
its conditioning order are unchanged.

The total number of indices in each class is unchanged, so the prescribed
offset domains are unchanged as well. Bounded modifications near a fixed
number of endpoints, turn anchors, or prime-anchor insertion buffers can
be reserved separately; their prefix-count contribution is still \(O(1)\).
Any such buffer treatment must keep its own good endpoint and occupancy
conditions. It is not supplied by the local swaps alone.

## 5. Order of construction and limits

The new physical class gap is at most nine instead of three. Choose the
constant geometric stride and the corresponding R2s bad-row definition
for that enlarged bound first. Then select a deterministic family known
to be good for that filter, thin it, sample the fields, and perform the
swaps. This prevents a circular definition in which changing the word
changes which rows were assumed good.

The method needs good anchors in the particular physical row pool being
partitioned. For example, \(22\mathcal A\) from
[the semigroup note](continuation_full_good_semigroup_gap_bounds.md)
occupies one fixed distance residue modulo four and can serve that pool.
An unproved fixed-residue gap estimate for another pool cannot be replaced
by a gap estimate for the union of two residues.

The separate [residue-gap adaptation](continuation_semigroup_residue_gap_tail.md)
now supplies the required moments for both odd residue classes of the
semigroup. Its source-based proof has passed an independent written
review. This does not make those rows good for an additional neighborhood
or interpolation filter: the anchor eligibility filter must still be
identified exactly before applying the planting theorem.

All original rows remain assigned to exactly one class, and the sampling
kernels survive the bounded-prefix modification. Completing the board
still requires the separately stated cap, P-choice, higher-support,
collar, and relocation arguments. No global construction follows merely
from the planting theorem.

## Review status

The disjoint-swap prefix identity and the bounded-error transfer were
derived independently before writing. The complete saved theorem passed
an independent written review, including ordered displacement matching,
uniform prefix control, thinning and alternation moments, raw-run
transfer, strip conditioning, and construction order. The explicit
endpoint-run condition in Section 1 records a clarification from that
review; the actual count-function words satisfy it.
A second independent reader also checked the prefix bound, displacement
matching, gap-nine bound, adaptive bounded-error transfer, thinning,
and alternating moment transfer.
