# A multiplicative family of R2s-good rows

15 November 2026. Every positive sea distance with no prime factor
congruent to 1 modulo 4 excludes the positive-offset R2s family. This
is stronger than the large-prime-factor criterion. It supplies a
multiplicative good subset, which matters for short-interval estimates.
Two smaller explicit subsets also exclude the negative-offset family.
No bound on good-row gaps or completed blackout is asserted here.

## 1. Positive offsets

Use the notation and geometric hypotheses of
[the explicit good-row note](continuation_explicit_r2s_good_rows.md).
For positive `delta`, reflect horizontally to make `U,T>0`. The exact
equations are

`TU=n delta`,
`V^2=U^2-2TU-delta^2`,
`2X=U+T-V`.                                                (1)

Here `n=y-s>0`, `delta` is a positive multiple of four, `T` is even,
and `U` is not divisible by four. The refined geometry gives
`delta^2<=2|X|`, except possibly `|X|=delta=4`.

**Theorem.** If every odd prime factor of `n` is congruent to 3 modulo
4, then (1) has no support satisfying these conditions.

Since `V^2>=0`, (1) implies `U>2T`. Moreover

`(U-T)^2-V^2=T^2+delta^2>0`.

Thus `|V|<U-T`, and consequently `0<X<U`. In the ordinary geometric
case this implies

`delta^2<2U`.                                             (2)

Write `g=gcd(U,delta)`, `U=g u`, `delta=g d`, where `g,u,d` are
positive and `gcd(u,d)=1`. Since `U` has 2-adic valuation at most one,
whereas `delta` has valuation at least two, `u` is odd and `d` is even.
The divisibility `U|n delta` shows `u|n`; put `n=u a`.

Fix any prime `p|u`. By hypothesis `p=3 mod4`. Write

`alpha=v_p(u)>0`, `gamma=v_p(g)`, `beta=v_p(n)>=alpha`.

If `gamma<beta`, the three terms in the norm equation have valuations

`v_p(U^2)=2gamma+2alpha`,
`v_p(2n delta)=beta+gamma`,
`v_p(delta^2)=2gamma`,

because `p` does not divide `d`. The first two valuations are strictly
larger than the third. Hence `v_p(V)=gamma`, and division by
`p^(2gamma)` makes the norm equation a nonzero square equal to a
negative nonzero square modulo `p`. This contradicts the quadratic
nonresiduosity of minus one. Therefore `gamma>=beta>=alpha`.

Applying this to every prime dividing the odd integer `u` gives `u|g`.
But (2), divided by `g`, says

`g d^2<2u`.

The left side is at least `4u`, since `g>=u` and `d` is even.
This contradiction proves the ordinary case.

The exceptional seam is impossible for positive delta, independently
of the factorization of `n`. Indeed `X=delta=4` gives
`V=U+T-8`. Substitution in (1) yields

`4(T-4)U+(T-8)^2+16=0`.                                  (3)

For positive even `T>=4` the left side is positive. For `T=2` it is
`-8U+52`, which has no integer zero. This completes the theorem.

## 2. Negative offsets for low seas

Suppose `1<=s<=4`. The white row `s+delta/2` forces any negative
offset to be `delta=-4`. Keep the no-split-prime hypothesis on `n`.
Then

`TU=-4n`, `V^2=U^2+8n-16`.                                (4)

If an odd prime `p` divides `U`, then it divides `n` and is 3 modulo
4. Reducing (4) modulo `p` gives `V^2=-16 modp`, impossible.
Thus `|U|` is a power of two. Since `U` is not divisible by four,

`|U|=1 or 2`.                                             (5)

The two possible square equations are therefore

`V^2=8n-15`, or `(V/2)^2=2n-3`.                           (6)

This is an exact reduction of all negative-offset supports for this
arithmetic family, not a count of them.

If additionally `n=1 mod3`, both equations of (6) are impossible
modulo three: their right sides are 2. Thus this congruence alone
removes the entire negative-offset family.

Let `A` be the multiplicative semigroup generated by primes congruent
to 7 modulo 12, including the empty product. Every element of `A`
is odd, is 1 modulo three, and has no split prime factor. The same
no-split and modulo-three properties hold for `22A`, since the odd
prime factor 11 is 3 modulo four and `22=1 mod3`.

For an odd sea, these give fully R2s-good rows as follows, subject
to the remaining bulk, height, and half-strip conditions:

* Distances `n` in `A` give even inserted rows.
* Distances `n` in `22A` give odd inserted rows, with `n=2 mod4`.

The even-row family contains both odd distance residues modulo four;
choosing a prescribed residue is an additional condition when used
inside one profile. No uniform distribution statement for that
condition is included here. The odd-row family automatically has the
required distance residue 2 modulo four.

Reflection gives the corresponding statements at high seas whose
reflected distance from the strip edge is at most four. A reflected
sea can be even; the arithmetic exclusion itself does not depend on
the sea's parity.

## 3. Scope and review

The positive-offset family is the multiplicative set of integers
having no prime factor 1 modulo four. The fully good even-row family
is another multiplicative set. The fully good odd-row family is a
fixed dilation of it. A short-interval theorem for membership in
these sets can therefore be relevant directly, rather than through
the complement of a multiplicative set.

This observation supplies no short-interval estimate by itself. Nor
does it assert that every good row belongs to one of these sparse
subsets. These families are prospective filler or local-gap inputs;
they cannot replace the required complete set of double labels.

Independent review: the positive-offset theorem, including the
valuation descent and exceptional seam, passed a separate written
derivation. A second derivation also supplied and checked the
negative-offset reduction, the modulo-three exclusion, and the
fully good families `A` and `22A`. Fixed-residue short-interval
estimates remain separate from this algebraic review.
