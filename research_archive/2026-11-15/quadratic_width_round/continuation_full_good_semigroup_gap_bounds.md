# Fully good inert semigroups and their gap moments

15 November 2026. There is a multiplicative family of completely R2s-good
distances from a low sea. The short-interval theorem for multiplicative
sets gives gap moments of every fixed order below \(3/2\). This
improves a physical squared-bad-run upper bound to every exponent above
\(4/3\), but not to \(1+o(1)\).

## 1. The positive-offset criterion needs no large prime

Let \(n=y-s>0\) have no prime factor congruent to one modulo four.
Use the mixed-support equations
\[
 TU=n\delta,\qquad V^2=U^2-2n\delta-\delta^2,
 \qquad 2X=U+T-V.                                    \tag{1}
\]
Assume \(\delta>0\), \(\delta\in4\mathbb Z\), \(T\) even,
\(U\notin4\mathbb Z\), and the reviewed same-parity geometric
bound \(\delta^2\le2|X|\), apart from the bounded seam
\(|X|=\delta=4\).

Reflect horizontally to make \(U,T>0\). Nonnegativity of \(V^2\)
gives \(U>2T\), and \(|V|<U-T\), so
\[
                              0<X<U.                \tag{2}
\]
Put \(g=\gcd(U,\delta)\), \(U=gu\), and \(\delta=gd\).
Then \((u,d)=1\), \(n=ua\), and \(T=ad\) for an integer
\(a>0\). Here \(u\) is odd and \(d\) is even. Every prime
\(p\mid u\) is therefore three modulo four. Write
\(\alpha=v_p(u)\), \(\gamma=v_p(g)\), and
\(\beta=v_p(n)\ge\alpha\). If \(\gamma<\beta\), reducing
the norm equation after dividing by \(p^{2\gamma}\) gives
\[
                         (V/p^\gamma)^2\equiv-d^2\pmod p,
\]
which is impossible because \(p\nmid d\). Thus
\(\gamma\ge\beta\ge\alpha\) for every prime of \(u\),
and \(u\mid g\). Equations (2) and the geometric bound yield
\(gd^2<2u\), contradicted by \(g\ge u\) and \(d^2\ge4\).

The seam also fails. There \(X=4\), \(\delta=4\), and (1)
reduces to
\[
                  4(T-4)U+(T-8)^2+16=0.
\]
For even \(T\ge4\) the left side is positive, while \(T=2\)
would require \(U=13/2\). This proves positive-offset goodness.

## 2. Remove the negative offset by a residue condition

For \(1\le s\le4\), the other white row \(s+\delta/2\)
allows only \(\delta=-4\) among negative multiples of four.
Then \(U\mid4n\). If an odd prime \(p\mid U\), it divides
\(n\) and is three modulo four; the norm equation gives
\(V^2\equiv-16\pmod p\), impossible. Since \(U\notin4\mathbb Z\),
only \(|U|=1,2\) remain.

If also \(n\equiv1\pmod3\), these are impossible:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 |U|&\text{necessary square}\\ \hline
 1&V^2=8n-15\equiv2\pmod3,\\
 2&(V/2)^2=2n-3\equiv2\pmod3.
 \end{array}
\]
Thus every positive \(n\equiv1\pmod3\) with no split prime
factor is fully R2s-good under the stated geometry.

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the multiplicative semigroup generated by
primes \(p\equiv7\pmod{12}\), including one. Every member of
\(\mathcal A\), and every member of \(22\mathcal A\), satisfies
this complete criterion. The factor \(22=2\cdot11\) introduces
no split prime and is one modulo three.

The residue distinction matters. The set \(22\mathcal A\) lies
entirely in \(2\pmod4\), so for an odd sea it gives good odd
rows in the required opposite residue. The set \(\mathcal A\)
contains both odd residues modulo four. Restricting it to one of those
residues is not a multiplicative-set operation; the direct theorem
used below does not make that restriction.

## 3. Apply the multiplicative-set gap theorem

The primary input is Corollary 1.2 of
[Matomäki--Radziwiłł, *Multiplicative functions in short intervals II*,
arXiv:2007.04290v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.04290).
Its prime nonvanishing condition holds for \(\mathcal A\) with
prime density \(1/4\), by the prime number theorem in the fixed
progression \(7\pmod{12}\). Its density parameter is
\[
 \Delta(X)=\prod_{p\le X,\ p\notin\mathcal A}(1-1/p)
                          \asymp(\log X)^{-3/4}.    \tag{3}
\]
Consequently, if \(a_1<a_2<\cdots\) enumerate \(\mathcal A\),
then for every fixed \(1\le\gamma<3/2\),
\[
 \sum_{a_j\le X}(a_{j+1}-a_j)^\gamma
       \ll_\gamma X(\log X)^{(3/4)(\gamma-1)}.       \tag{4}
\]
The same bound, with a changed constant, holds for the gaps of
\(22\mathcal A\). Fixed translations and fixed linear rescalings
of a row-index pool also preserve it.

For clarity, the associated empty-interval consequence can be written
with the normalized length \(u=t\Delta(X)\): except for
\(O_\varepsilon(Xu^{-1/2+\varepsilon})\) starts in \([X,2X]\),
an interval of length \(t\) contains at least a fixed positive
multiple of \(u\) members of \(\mathcal A\), provided \(u\ge2\).
For \(22\mathcal A\), replace \(u\) by
\((t/22)\Delta(X/22)\). These are statements about the uncut
semigroup or its fixed scale, not a fixed odd residue of \(\mathcal A\).

## 4. Maximum gaps and physical bad-run energy

Equation (4) bounds every individual gap up to \(X\) by
\[
                 O_\gamma\bigl(
                    X^{1/\gamma}(\log X)^{3(\gamma-1)/(4\gamma)}
                              \bigr).               \tag{5}
\]
It also gives a squared-gap bound without claiming a second moment
in the source theorem. Since the \(\ell^2\) norm is at most the
\(\ell^\gamma\) norm for \(\gamma<2\),
\[
 \sum_{a_j\le X}(a_{j+1}-a_j)^2
   \le\left(\sum_{a_j\le X}(a_{j+1}-a_j)^\gamma\right)^{2/\gamma}
   \ll_\gamma X^{2/\gamma}
                  (\log X)^{3(\gamma-1)/(2\gamma)}. \tag{6}
\]
The same assertions hold for the scaled and translated family.

If a physical bad-row set avoids that good family, each of its runs
lies in one family gap. The sum of squared lengths of all bad runs
inside a single gap is at most the square of that gap. Hence (6)
also bounds its physical squared-run energy. In the fixed row residue
supplied by \(22\mathcal A\), this proves, for every fixed
\(\eta>0\),
\[
                    \sum\ell_j^2\ll_\eta X^{4/3+\eta}.          \tag{7}
\]
It does not prove \(X^{1+o(1)}\). Endpoint or bulk cuts may be
bracketed by the neighboring elements of the full family; they introduce
no larger gap than already counted. Additional arithmetic filters that
remove members of the family need a separate argument.

## 5. Sampled classes need a transfer

A physical good-row gap bound alone does not bound gaps in a sampled
class. A class with bounded jumps can skip isolated good rows. Therefore
(4)--(7) do not automatically apply to the raw bad runs of the mechanical
or multiscale partitions.

The bounded-prefix-error planting construction in
[the balanced-anchor note](continuation_balanced_good_anchor_planting.md)
provides a separate transfer when a deterministic good family is
available in the physical row pool. It preserves coverage and the
sampling kernels after enlarging their fixed bounded errors. The
geometric stride and the deterministic bad-row filter must be chosen
for the resulting enlarged adjacent-gap constant before that
construction is used.

## Review status

The nonsplit-prime positive-offset argument and the negative-offset
residue criterion were derived and checked independently. Corollary 1.2
was read in the original paper. The complete saved gap consequences
passed independent written review: the source exponent and logarithmic
power, fixed scaling, maximum-gap and norm bounds, containment of bad
runs, endpoints, and residue/extra-filter qualifications were checked
again. No fixed-odd-residue extension is assumed here.
