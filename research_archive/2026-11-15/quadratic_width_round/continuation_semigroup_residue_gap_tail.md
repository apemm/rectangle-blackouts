# Strong gap tails in the two odd residue classes of the good semigroup

15 November 2026. This is a derived adaptation of the
Matomäki--Radziwiłł argument, not a direct invocation of its
multiplicative-set corollary. The signed main term and both interval
regimes are checked below. The complete adaptation passed an independent
written review against the original v1 paper.

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the semigroup generated by primes
\(p\equiv7\pmod {12}\), including \(1\), and put
\[
 \mathcal A_\sigma=\{n\in\mathcal A:\chi_4(n)=\sigma\},
 \qquad \sigma\in\{1,-1\}.
\]
Thus these are respectively the \(1\) and \(3\pmod4\) parts. Set
\[
 \Delta(X)=\prod_{\substack{p\le X\\p\not\equiv7\ (12)}}
                    (1+1/p)
             \asymp(\log X)^{3/4}.                         \tag{1}
\]
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), there are positive constants
\(c_\varepsilon,C_\varepsilon\) such that, uniformly for
\(2\le u\le X/(2\Delta(X))\), the number of integer \(x\in[X,2X]\)
for which
\[
 \#\bigl(\mathcal A_\sigma\cap(x,x+u\Delta(X)]\bigr)
                         <c_\varepsilon u                 \tag{2}
\]
is at most
\[
                  C_\varepsilon X u^{-1/2+\varepsilon}.
                                                               \tag{3}
\]
The constants are uniform in the two signs.

If \(a_1<a_2<\cdots\) enumerates either class, then for every fixed
\(1\le\gamma<3/2\),
\[
 \sum_{a_j\le X}(a_{j+1}-a_j)^\gamma
                 \ll_\gamma X(\log X)^{3(\gamma-1)/4}.       \tag{4}
\]
The reverse bound follows from the usual mean upper bound and Hölder,
but only the upper bound is needed here. Fixed dilations and translations
preserve (3)--(4), with changed constants. No second-moment conclusion
\(\gamma=2\) is asserted.

## 1. Source inputs and the exact signed decomposition

The primary source is [Matomäki and Radziwiłł, *Multiplicative functions
in short intervals II*, arXiv:2007.04290v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.04290v1).
We use its Definition 1.8, Lemmas 5.1(iii), 5.3(i), 9.1,
Theorem 9.2(i),(iii), Proposition 12.1(i),(ii), and the positive
mean estimates in the proof of Theorem 1.11. Its proof of Corollary 1.2
supplies the final elementary gap-tail integration.

Define the completely multiplicative functions
\[
 f_0=1_{\mathcal A},\qquad f_1=f_0\chi_4.
\]
Their prime values are
\[
 f_0(p)=1,\quad f_1(p)=-1 \quad(p\equiv7\pmod {12}),
 \qquad f_0(p)=f_1(p)=0\quad\hbox{otherwise}.                \tag{5}
\]
In particular \(|f_1|=f_0\), both are real, and
\[
                 1_{\mathcal A_\sigma}=(f_0+\sigma f_1)/2.
                                                               \tag{6}
\]
The prime number theorem in this fixed progression gives the
nonvanishing hypothesis with any fixed \(\alpha<1/4\) and fixed
\(\theta>0\). Their common source normalization is exactly
\(H(f_0;X)=H(f_1;X)=\Delta(X)\). Write
\[
 d(X)=\prod_{p\le X}\left(1+\frac{f_0(p)-1}{p}\right).
 \qquad d(X)\Delta(X)\asymp1.                              \tag{7}
\]

## 2. Uniform signed distance and the sieve-weighted mean

There is an absolute \(c>0\) such that
\[
                    \widehat M(f_1;X)\ge c\log\log X.       \tag{8}
\]
Indeed, for \(|t|\le(\log X)^{-1/2}\), restrict the distance sum
to allowed primes \(p\le\exp(\sqrt{\log X})\). On these primes
\(|t\log p|\le1\), so each summand has numerator
\(1+\cos(t\log p)\ge1+\cos1\). Their reciprocal sum is
\((1/8+o(1))\log\log X\). For larger \(|t|\le X\),
Lemma 5.1(iii), applied to the real function \(f_1\), gives (8):
its minimum involving \(3\log(1+|t|\log X)\) is then at least
\(\log\log X\). The fixed nonvanishing parameters make the constant
uniform in \(t\).

An important detail is that the excluded-prime version of Lemma 5.3(i)
uses the distance of the original function \(f_1\), not that of the
function obtained after deleting primes. Consequently, uniformly over
every set of excluded primes \(\mathcal P\),
\[
 \frac1X\left|\sum_{\substack{X<n\le2X\\
                        p\mid n\Rightarrow p\notin\mathcal P}}
                        f_1(n)\right|
                    \ll d(X)(\log X)^{-c_1}               \tag{9}
\]
for some fixed \(c_1>0\). The unweighted dyadic version in (9) is
proved explicitly at the start of that lemma's proof; no reverse
partial-summation inference is needed.

Let \(\mathcal S\) be the source's sieve set used in the small-\(u\)
proof of Theorem 1.11. It requires a prime divisor in each of
\(J+2\) prescribed bands. Those bands satisfy
\[
             J=O\!\left(\frac{\log\log X}
                                  {\log\log\log X}\right).
                                                               \tag{10}
\]
Apply the exact inclusion--exclusion identity of Lemma 9.1 to (9).
There are \(2^{J+2}=(\log X)^{o(1)}\) terms, each of which is
an excluded-prime sum. It follows that
\[
       X^{-1}\sum_{\substack{X<n\le2X\\n\in\mathcal S}}f_1(n)
                                  =o(d(X)).                \tag{11}
\]
This establishes the signed mean uniformly over the permissible
band choices. In particular, a sieve restriction is not silently
discarded when using cancellation.

## 3. The small normalized intervals

Fix a sufficiently small parameter \(\epsilon>0\), chosen below the
desired \(\varepsilon\). Use exactly the bands in the small-\(u\)
proof of Theorem 1.11:
\[
 Q_1=u,\quad P_1=u^{1-\epsilon/1000},\quad
 \nu_2=\epsilon/3,\quad
 \nu_1=\nu_2(1-\epsilon/1000),\quad \eta=\epsilon/1000.
                                                               \tag{12}
\]
The later bands and the fixed auxiliary parameter are as in that
proof. Its positive argument applies to \(f_0\) without alteration:
\[
 X^{-1}\sum_{\substack{X<n\le2X\\n\in\mathcal S}}f_0(n)
                            \ge c_\epsilon d(X).            \tag{13}
\]
Together (11), (13), and (6) give a positive main term for each sign.

Apply Theorem 9.2(i),(iii) separately to \(f_0\) and \(f_1\),
with the same \(\mathcal S\) and interval length \(u\Delta(X)\).
Choose its fixed error tolerance smaller than \(c_\epsilon/20\).
Its real-function clause permits the untwisted means in
(11)--(13). Outside the union of the two exceptional sets, the
short sum of \(1_{\mathcal A_\sigma}1_{\mathcal S}\) is
\(\gg_\epsilon u\), and the unrestricted class count is larger.

For completeness, the two exceptional-set terms are bounded as follows.
The first is, up to a constant,
\[
 X(\log u)^2
       u^{-(1-\epsilon/1000)(1/2-\epsilon/3-3\epsilon/1000)}
                    \ll_\epsilon X u^{-1/2+\epsilon}.
                                                               \tag{14}
\]
For \(u\le X^{\epsilon^3/20000}\), the second,
\(X^{1-\nu_1^3/200}\), is also at most this quantity.
This follows from
\(\nu_1^3/200>(\epsilon^3/20000)(1/2-\epsilon)\) for small
\(\epsilon\). Bounded \(u\), before the source's bands become
admissible, are covered by increasing the constant in (3).

## 4. The large normalized intervals

Suppose \(u>X^{\epsilon^3/20000}\), and put
\(H=u\Delta(X)\le X/2\). Use the prime band and fixed integer \(k\)
in Proposition 12.1, and let
\[
 W_i(x,H)=\frac1H
 \sum_{\substack{x<p_1\cdots p_k m\le x+H\\
                  p_1,\ldots,p_k\in\mathcal P,\ m\ge1}}
                          f_i(p_1\cdots p_km).              \tag{15}
\]
The source's positive convolution estimate in its proof of
Theorem 1.11, followed by Proposition 12.1(i), gives
\(W_0(x,H)\ge c_\epsilon d(X)\) outside
\(O_\epsilon(XH^{-1/2+\epsilon})\) exceptions.
Proposition 12.1(ii), now using (8), gives
\(|W_1(x,H)|=o(d(X))\) outside another set of that size.
Thus \((W_0+\sigma W_1)/2\gg_\epsilon d(X)\) for both signs.

This is a nonnegative weighted count of \(\mathcal A_\sigma\).
Every prime in \(\mathcal P\) exceeds a fixed power of \(X\).
An integer at most \(3X\) therefore has only \(O_\epsilon(1)\)
prime factors from that band, counted with multiplicity, and hence
only \(O_\epsilon(1)\) ordered representations in (15). Dividing
by this bound turns the weighted lower bound into (2).
Finally \(H\ge u\) makes the exceptional count no larger than (3).
This proves the large-\(u\) regime without assuming that
\(\mathcal A_\sigma\) itself is multiplicative.

## 5. Gap moments and the geometric scope

The empty-interval consequence of (3) gives, for gaps \(g_j\) whose
left endpoints lie in a fixed dyadic interval,
\[
 \sum_{g_j\ge w}g_j
       \ll_\varepsilon X\left(\frac{w}{\Delta(X)}\right)
                                         ^{-1/2+\varepsilon},
 \qquad w\ge C_\varepsilon\Delta(X).                        \tag{16}
\]
One uses the middle part of each empty gap and intervals of length
\(w/2\); changes of dyadic interval alter only constants. At lengths
comparable to \(X\), (3) also excludes an empty interval of that
size, since such an interval would supply \(\gg X\) exceptional
starting points. The gap tails therefore stay inside comparable
dyadic ranges. Integrating (16) against \(w^{\gamma-2}\), with
\(\varepsilon<3/2-\gamma\), proves (4); gaps shorter than
\(C_\varepsilon\Delta(X)\) contribute
\(O_\gamma(X\Delta(X)^{\gamma-1})\).

The arithmetic good-row criterion is proved separately in
[the multiplicative-good-row note](continuation_multiplicative_r2s_good_rows.md).
The present adaptation supplies the missing fixed-residue distribution
input for \(\mathcal A_+\) and \(\mathcal A_-\), as well as for
fixed dilates such as \(22\mathcal A\). It does not assert that a
row set remaining after an arbitrary further deletion has the same
gap bound. Any transfer into a sampled class must preserve the chosen
good anchors explicitly.

The range \(\gamma<3/2\) remains essential. In particular, (4) does
not establish squared run energy \(O(X)\), a uniformly bounded gap,
or a completed quadratic-width blackout construction.

## Review record

An independent reader checked the original v1 PDF and the complete
written argument. The review covered the uniform signed distance,
excluded-prime Halász bound, sieve-band count, both terms in the
small-interval exceptional set, the two prime-convolution estimates,

assertion that arbitrary arithmetic-progression restrictions follow
