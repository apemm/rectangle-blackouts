# A filled join between two separated-row tiles

Take two of the prime representative blocks in [the separated-row construction](separated_row_tiles.md). Their primes and slopes can differ. Write \(d\ge1\) for an upper bound on the span of all their low rows and, separately, on the span of all their high rows. Here all low rows have the same physical parity and all high rows have the other parity. Let \(g\) be the distance between the two convex hulls. Place the blocks in order. Insert \(D=4d^2+1\) columns of the left block's high representative, and then \(D\) columns of the right block's low representative. In each strip use the periodic representative of its adjoining block, with the phase adjusted for its physical location. Let \(S\) be the full horizontal span including both guards. Assume
\[
 g>\max(2S^2,S+d).
\]
The combined interval is filled. All legitimate doubles of the two original blocks survive. It admits no tilted black corner triple, mixed nonvertical support or in-strip parity rival. For local validity require distinct prescribed double labels over the two blocks. Any single endpoints deleted from the original tiles cause no problem.

**Proof.** Within a layer the two different representative systems are separated by at least \(D\) intervening columns. Inside either system all nonhorizontal chords have nonintegral length by the prime nonresidue calculation. If a nonhorizontal chord crosses the separator, \(0<Y^2\le d^2<2|X|+1\), so its squared norm lies strictly between adjacent squares. Thus no nonhorizontal within-layer chord has integer length.

Each layer has at most one point in a column, and its total row span is at most \(d\). Hence any two of its points satisfy \(|\Delta y|\le d|\Delta x|\). The [linear locality argument](linear_collar_locality.md) bounds the span of a tilted black triple in that layer by \(2d^2\), and of a mixed support by \(3d^2\). Its proof uses the vertical differences of the black points, so it applies even if the common corners or the fourth corner are outside the layer's row interval. Each monochromatic support must lie in one representative system. That system has no such support by the prime proof.

If a tilted triple uses both layers, the product of its vertical leg magnitudes is at least \(g\), larger than its horizontal product. For mixed supports write \(U\) for the side vector, \(V\) for the diagonal vector and \(H\) for twice the displacement of their midpoints. The horizontal component of \(U\) is nonzero. One cross-layer chord and one within-layer chord cannot have equal length since \(g>S+d\). If both cross, \(U\cdot H=0\) and \(|U_y|>2S^2\ge |U_xH_x|\) force \(H_y=0\) and then \(H_x=0\). If the two chords are internal to different layers then \(|H_y|\ge2g\), so the same equation forces a horizontal side. The diagonal must then also be horizontal by the integer-length exclusion. Equal horizontal vectors up to sign give a degenerate mixed pair.

Every nonvertical chord joining opposite-parity rows crosses between layers. Its squared norm lies strictly between \(Y^2\) and \((|Y|+1)^2\), because \(0<X^2\le S^2<2|Y|+1\). This handles arbitrary remote vertical owners. Together with distinct row pairs in a bipartite graph, the tests imply validity of the combined block.

**Cost.** For neighboring side-\(b\) tiles with both layer spans \(O(b)\), the guards cost \(O(b^2)\), the same order as the tile width. A sufficiently large constant in the quadtree stopping rule supplies the displayed separation after the two guards are included. The argument also joins any fixed number of tiles with those same aggregate row-span and gap hypotheses by using the same two alternating guards at every boundary. All separated representatives of a given layer then have a gap exceeding its local obstruction span. The resulting theorem depends on the total horizontal span \(S\), so arbitrarily many joins require a further argument.

**Why the guards are needed.** Use primes 3 and 5 with both slopes 1, and keep the five even rows \(500,502,504,506,508\) in both blocks. The first block uses odd rows \(1,3,5\) at columns 0 through 14; the second uses \(7,9,11\) at columns 15 through 29. In a column \(x\) choose its odd row congruent to \(x\bmod3\) and its even row congruent to \(x\bmod5\). Each layer gap exceeds \(2\cdot14^2\). Across the join the black corners \((13,1),(16,7),(9,3)\) leave only \((12,9)\) visible. The axis rectangle on columns 12 and 27 with rows 9 and 502 gives the same presentation. Thus even matching primes, matching slopes, matching phases, and one identical row layer do not suffice for an immediate join.
