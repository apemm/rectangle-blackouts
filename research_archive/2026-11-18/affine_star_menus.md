# Fixed menus of affine columns for a star

Fix an odd center row \(o\). A leaf in even row \(o+t\) has odd offset \(t\). Consider bands where the physical column of that leaf has the form \(b+kt\), with \(b\) constant within the band and \(k\) a nonzero integer. The intercept may depend on the star, height and band.

**Theorem.** Fix a finite menu of nonzero integer slopes and an upper bound on the number of bands allowed in a star. For all sufficiently large \(h\), each odd \(o\in[h/3,h/2]\) needs at least \(\eta h\) positive-offset leaves outside such bands if the placement avoids true in-strip vertical rivals. The constant \(\eta>0\) depends on the menu and band bound. Thus deleting \(o(h^2)\) labels from the whole board cannot make this construction work on all central stars. The restriction also holds for a bounded *average* number of bands per star if the budget counts all stars. Nonlinear permutations in the [star placement](star_placement.md) are not subject to this restriction.

## Recurrence for each slope

For an integer \(k\ge1\), join two positive odd integers \(u<v\) when \(u^2+k^2(v-u)^2\) is a square. Every finite coloring has a monochromatic edge. Here are the needed changes to the [parity-localized correlation proof](../2026-11-15/quadratic_width_round/continuation_odd_p_localized_partition_regularity.md). They use the same published correlation and concentration theorems. [Frantzikinakis, Klurman and Moreira](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08360v3)

Take
\[
 R(m,n)=(m-kn)(m+kn),\qquad T(m,n)=R(m,n)+2mn.
\]
Then \(R^2+k^2(T-R)^2=(m^2+k^2n^2)^2\). The linear factors are independent, and \(T\) is irreducible because \(k^2+1\) lies strictly between consecutive squares. At exceptional primes, now including those dividing \(2k\), use the local offsets \((a,b)=(1,0)\) to the full prime-power modulus. At every other prime use \(b=1,\ a=k+p^{\ell_p}\). The factors \(a-kb\) and \(a+kb\) then have valuations \(\ell_p\) and zero, and \(T(a,b)\equiv2k\not\equiv0\bmod p\). Thus the same divisor \(c=\prod p^{\ell_p}\) is removed from the first linear form; the second form and the quadratic form are coprime to the modulus. Both full forms are 1 modulo the exceptional conductor \(r!\).

Use the positive cone \(m>kn\) with the real logarithmic ratio weight of the cited argument. The ratio \(T/R=1+2z/(1-k^2z^2)\), \(z=n/m\), increases from one on \(0\le z<1/k\). The weight can be supported in a smaller cone \(z\le z_0<1/k\); after the CRT shifts at most \(O_k(1)\) initial \(m\)-slices need be dropped to ensure \(Qm+a>k(Qn+b)\). This has zero limiting density and preserves positivity. For the aperiodic estimate the two linear forms and the irreducible quadratic satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem 3.1. For the concentration estimates in Propositions 4.7 and 4.9, reduce the offsets of \(m\pm kn\) to the standard range by shifts of size \(O_k(1)\), after removing \(c\) from the negative-sign factor. A sum over either linear form has multiplicity at most \(N\) at each integer and range \(O_k(N)\). The comparison of the phase sums at \(N\) and \((k+1)N\) follows from the same pretentious-distance tail estimate. The root count of \(T\) is at most two outside the finitely many bad primes. These changes preserve the two concentration formulas, their character factors and the order of their limits. The geometric average over \(c\) and the final spectral positivity argument of the cited proof therefore apply. The shifted parameters have first coordinate odd and second even, and both form values are odd. Removing the power of two in their common dilate gives the claimed recurrence on positive odd integers. Its finite consequence is that for every \(D\) some finite graph of this kind has chromatic number greater than \(D\).

## Permitting a different slope for each leaf

List the positive magnitudes of the allowed slopes as \(k_1,\ldots,k_s\), and fix a palette of \(C\) band names. There is a finite set \(S\) of positive odd integers such that every \(C\)-coloring of \(S\) contains a monochromatic set with an edge for *each* of the \(s\) slopes. To construct it, work backward. Choose a finite graph \(F_s\) for slope \(k_s\) of chromatic number greater than \(C\). For \(j=s-1,\ldots,1\) choose \(F_j\) for slope \(k_j\) with chromatic number greater than \(C^{\prod_{i>j}|F_i|}\). Take \(S\) to be the products of one vertex from each graph.

Pull the coloring of \(S\) back to the product. At the first coordinate, color a vertex by its vector of color values over all remaining coordinates. Two adjacent vertices have the same vector. Retain their edge and fix one endpoint. Repeat in the remaining coordinates. All \(2^s\) corners of the resulting box have one color. Varying only coordinate \(j\) multiplies a slope-\(k_j\) edge by an odd integer, so its two values in \(S\) are distinct and still give a slope-\(k_j\) edge. Coincidences between products at other corners do not affect this argument.

Color each retained offset at a central star by its band name. Each band has a single slope magnitude. On a full odd dilate \(dS\) the monochromatic set would include an offending pair \(t<u\) of the slope assigned to that very band. In that band the center point of the column at offset \(u\) and the leaf point of the column at offset \(t\) have squared distance \(L^2=t^2+k^2(u-t)^2\).

Let \(M\) exceed every element of \(S\) and every edge length in the finite witness, for all the slopes used there. Choose odd \(1\le d\le h/(10M)\). Then the positive row offsets and the scaled lengths are at most \(h/10\). The vertical alternative has rows \(o+(t-L)/2\) and \(o+(t+L)/2\), both in the strip. Its column is integral since the two offsets are odd and the horizontal difference is even. Its rows have opposite parity since \(L\) is odd. On a complete parity board this forces a collision. If the row pair is owned elsewhere, compare the axis rectangle from that owner with the tilted rectangle on the two diagonals. If the common column owns the pair, all four tilted corners are black, which is already impossible. If no complete-board assumption is made, the pair still violates the strip-rival condition itself.

There are \(\gg_S h\) eligible dilates. A single discarded offset lies in at most \(|S|\) of them. Hence each such star needs \(\gg_S h\) discarded leaves. There are \(\gg h\) central stars. For an \(O(h)\) total budget of bands, choose a fixed \(C\) large enough that at least half of the central stars have at most \(C\) bands. Summation gives the asserted quadratic deletion bound.

The theorem excludes fixed finite menus of affine columns applied separately to horizontal stars. The stride-menu draft uses parallel diagonals pairing *two moving rows*, so its bound is unaffected.
